# Fuzhou Historic Houses — Heritage Knowledge Base (English)

## 1. Quarters

### Three Lanes and Seven Alleys (Sanfang Qixiang)
In the heart of the old city, centred on Nanhou Street. West side: Yijin, Wenru and Guanglu lanes; east side: Yangqiao, Languan, Ta, Huang, Anmin, Gong and Jibi alleys. It preserves 159 Ming and Qing buildings and is called a "living fossil of the ward system" and a "museum of Ming-Qing architecture". It took shape in the Jin and Tang periods and was long home to scholar-officials.
- Lin Zexu Memorial Hall: built 1905, with an imperial stele pavilion.
- Yan Fu's former residence in Languan Alley: his later home, a Chinese-Western hybrid.
- Lin Juemin and Bing Xin former residence in Yangqiao Alley: two famous residents, one courtyard; Lin wrote "A Letter to My Wife" here.
- Ermei Book House, the Water Pavilion Opera Stage in Yijin Lane, Little Yellow Tower in Huang Alley, Shen Baozhen's residence in Gong Alley.

### Shangxiahang
The old Min River wharf district, packed with guild halls — "Fuzhou's museum of traditional commerce". Key sites: Caifeng Villa, Gutian Guild Hall, Yongde Guild Hall, Fuzhou Chamber of Commerce.

### Yantai Hill
Consulates, trading houses, churches and schools from the treaty-port era — "a museum of world architecture", a counterpoint to the Chinese houses of Sanfang Qixiang.

### Zhuzi Fang and Aofeng Fang
Zhuzi Fang was a Song-Ming official-residence quarter; Aofeng Fang held the Aofeng Academy, a centre of learning.

## 2. Architectural details

### Saddle wall (ma'an qiang)
A fire wall unique to Fuzhou. It rises and dips with the timber frame, both ends turning up and the middle dipping like a saddle. It blocks fire and gives Min architecture its flowing profile. Tops often carry grey plaster or painting and upturned corners. Difference from the Huizhou "horse-head wall": the saddle wall is one continuous curve, the horse-head wall steps down in tiers.

### Swallowtail ridge
Both ends of the main ridge fork upward like a swallow's tail, hence "swallowtail ridge". It symbolises swallows returning to the nest — family prosperity and the return of wanderers. Common on Minnan and coastal Fuzhou houses, often decorated with grey plaster, cut-and-paste porcelain and painting.

### Grey plaster (huisu / hui-pi)
Lime as the base, mixed with hemp fibre, glutinous rice paste and brown-sugar water for flexibility. Artisans model birds, flowers, figures, antique motifs and auspicious beasts on parapets, lintels, gable walls and screen walls. Fuzhou examples are restrained: blue-grey and off-white grounds with touches of vermilion and ochre. Popular themes: the birthday of Guo Ziyi, the Eight Immortals, squirrels and grapes, plum-orchid-bamboo-chrysanthemum.

### Wood carving and lattice windows
Halls use a hybrid post-and-beam / through-tenon frame; beams, brackets and lattice doors carry carving. Lattice patterns include ice-crack, ten-thousand (swastika), crabapple and "step-by-step brocade".

### Sky well and tashou
The sky well gives light, ventilation and drainage ("four waters return to the hall"). The recessed gateway, tashou, shelters the entrance and shows status.

### Gatehouse and cheng
The street-side first bay is the gatehouse; the open forecourt is the cheng, used for drying and gathering.

## 3. Min folk customs

- Aojiu Festival (29th of the first lunar month): aojiu porridge with glutinous rice, dates, peanuts, longan, water chestnut and red beans; married daughters send it home — "Filial Piety Festival".
- Beginning of summer: guobian hu (rice batter soup), with shrimp fritters and oyster cakes.
- Qixi: Fuzhou's "skill-begging" festival, eating broad beans to "form bonds".
- Mid-Autumn: tower displays, carp cakes, ritual cakes; Nanhou Street held a "tower market".
- Weddings: hair-pinning rite, bridal laments, stepping over a brazier, bowing rites, the matchmaker singing verses.
- Min opera, pinghua storytelling and chichang singing are the three great performing arts; bodiless lacquerware, Shoushan stone carving, cork painting and oil-paper umbrellas are the crafts; jasmine tea is the city's signature.

## 4. People and stories

- Lin Zexu: destroyed opium at Humen; returned to Fuzhou in old age and promoted West Lake's restoration.
- Yan Fu: translated "Evolution and Ethics" and coined "natural selection".
- Lin Juemin: one of the 72 martyrs of Huanghuagang; wrote "A Letter to My Wife" in Yangqiao Alley.
- Bing Xin: born in Fuzhou, childhood in the Yangqiao Alley house; later wrote "Hometown".
- Shen Baozhen: minister of the Fuzhou Navy Yard; residence in Gong Alley.
- Chen Baochen: tutor of the last emperor, from the Luo Zhou Chen clan, lectured at Aofeng Academy.
- Lin Huiyin: architect, Fuzhou ancestry, deep ties to Sanfang Qixiang.

## 5. Visiting tips

- Allow 3–4 hours for Sanfang Qixiang; mornings are quieter and raking light shows the saddle-wall curves.
- For grey plaster, look at gateways in Yijin and Wenru lanes; for swallowtail ridges, look up at the ends of the main ridge.
- Shangxiahang is best at dusk, when lighting brings out the guild-hall brick carving.
- Yantai Hill suits late afternoon into sunset, for treaty-port architecture and river views.
